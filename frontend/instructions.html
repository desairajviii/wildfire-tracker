Wildfire Tracker

How to use Wildfire Tracker

Wildfire Tracker shows US wildfires next to the places and trips you care about. You don't need an account. Your saved places, trips, and plans stay tied to this browser.

On this page

  • Browse fires
  • Read and post fire reports
  • Watch a place
  • Check a hiking trip
  • Make an evacuation plan
  • About the data
  • Your data and this browser

Browse fires

  1. Open Fires to see every fire in the dataset, newest first.
  2. Type a two-letter state code, like CA, and choose Apply filters.
  3. Check Active fires only to hide fires that are out.
  4. Use Previous and Next to page through results.
  5. Choose a fire's name to see its details and reports.

"Unknown" means the fire's size, date, or containment hasn't been reported yet. It doesn't mean zero.

Read and post fire reports

Reports are what people near a fire are seeing, like smoke, a road closure, or an evacuation.

  1. Open a fire from the Fires page, a watchlist, or a trip.
  2. Read the reports under Reports from people nearby.
  3. To add one, choose what you saw, describe it, and choose Post report.
  4. Your own reports have Edit and Delete buttons. You can't change anyone else's.

Anyone can read reports, and they aren't verified. For evacuation orders, always follow your local officials.

Watch a place

  1. Open Watchlists.
  2. Enter a name, the place's latitude and longitude, and how many miles around it to watch.
  3. Choose Save place.
  4. Choose View to see a map of the place and every active fire within your radius, nearest first.

Finding latitude and longitude

In Google Maps, right-click the spot. The first number in the menu is the latitude and the second is the longitude.

Check a hiking trip

  1. Open Trips.
  2. Enter the trail name, the trailhead's latitude and longitude, your dates, and how many miles around the trail to check.
  3. The list shows how many active fires are near each trip. Choose View for the map and the full list.

Fire checks show fires that are active now, not a forecast for your trip dates. Check again closer to your trip.

Make an evacuation plan

  1. Open Evacuation plans.
  2. Add your household name, a meeting point, up to 5 routes out, and the people in your household.
  3. Choose Copy share link on a plan and send it to your family. They can view the plan without the app, but only you can change it.

Anyone who has the link can view the plan, so only share it with people you trust.

About the data

Fire data comes from the National Interagency Fire Center (NIFC) and covers US wildfires from the last five years. The app updates it periodically, so the Fires page shows the date of the last update.

"Active" means the fire is on NIFC's current incident list. That can include long-running fires that are mostly contained, so check official sources for conditions on the ground.

Your data and this browser

There's no login. The app gives this browser a random ID and uses it to keep your places, trips, and plans separate from other people's.

  • If you clear your browser data or switch browsers, you'll lose access to what you saved.
  • This keeps your data separate, but it isn't real security. Don't enter real personal information.
